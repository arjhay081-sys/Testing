Best Restaurants & Food in Santa Fe, Bantayan Island 2026

Published March 2026 | Food Guide | 12 min read

Eating well on Bantayan Island does not require spending much money. Santa Fe has a growing food scene — from open-air seafood restaurants right on the beach to small carenderias serving honest Filipino home cooking for under ₱100. This guide covers where to eat, what to order, and how to eat like a local on Bantayan Island.

Local tip from Bobby's: The best eating on Bantayan Island is almost never in the tourist-facing places. Ask your tricycle driver or the staff at your guesthouse where they eat lunch. That place — the one with plastic chairs and no English menu — will be your best meal of the trip.

The Food Culture of Bantayan Island

Bantayan Island is a fishing community first and a tourist destination second. That fishing heritage shapes everything about the food here. The fish on your plate this evening was likely caught this morning — that freshness is something you can actually taste, and it is the single biggest difference between eating here and eating seafood almost anywhere else.

The dominant local food tradition is sutukil — a Cebuano concept combining suga (grilled), tuwa (soup/broth), and kilaw (cured raw fish). You'll find variations of this at most seafood spots around Santa Fe. Point at the fish you want from a displayed selection, choose how you want it cooked, and it comes out fresh.

Danggit — dried and salted rabbitfish — is Bantayan's most famous food export. You'll see it drying on racks in front of houses and small stalls throughout the island. It's eaten for breakfast, fried crispy, with rice and vinegar. If you leave Bantayan without trying danggit and without buying a pack to bring home, you have made a mistake.

Beachfront Seafood in Santa Fe

The main strip along Santa Fe beach has several open-air restaurants operating on the same basic concept: fresh fish and shellfish displayed on ice, you choose your fish, they cook it. Most have tables right on the sand or just meters from the water. Prices vary but expect to pay ₱150–400 per dish depending on the fish and portion size.

HOW TO ORDER SEAFOOD HERE

Most beachfront spots show you their fresh catch on ice. Point to what you want then say: sinugba (grilled), pinirito (fried), tinuwa (soup/broth), or kinilaw (raw, cured in vinegar). Grilled is the most popular and usually the best way to taste fresh fish here.

What to order:

  • Grilled tuna or tanigue — local staple, served with rice and sukang Iloco (local cane vinegar)
  • Kinilaw na tanigue — raw fish cured in vinegar with onion, ginger, and chili. Order this as a starter
  • Sinuglaw — grilled pork belly combined with kinilaw fish. Unusual combination but genuinely delicious
  • Grilled pusit (squid) — brushed with garlic butter, charred slightly, served whole
  • Steamed crab or shrimp — simple, direct, and usually very fresh

Budget guide: A full meal for two with rice, one fish dish, one shellfish, and drinks typically costs ₱400–700. Solo diners eating a single fish with rice and a drink can eat for ₱150–250.

NOTE ON SPECIFIC RESTAURANT NAMES

Restaurant names and businesses in Santa Fe change frequently — new places open, others close or rebrand. We recommend asking locals or your accommodation host which restaurants are currently open and recommended. The best source of up-to-date dining info on Bantayan is always a local, not an online guide.

Local Carenderias — Eat Like a Local

A carenderia is a small Filipino eatery where pre-cooked dishes are displayed in trays and you point to what you want — turo-turo style ("point-point"). These are where locals eat every day and they are almost always the best value food on any Philippine island.

In Santa Fe, small carenderias are scattered along the main road and in the barangay interior. They typically serve lunch from around 10:30 AM until food runs out — usually by 1:30 or 2:00 PM. Go early for the best selection.

Common dishes:

  • Tinolang manok — chicken soup with ginger, green papaya, and malunggay leaves
  • Adobong baboy or manok — pork or chicken cooked in vinegar, soy sauce, garlic, and bay leaves. Excellent over white rice
  • Pinangat na isda — fish in a sour broth with kamias (bilimbi fruit). Distinctly local flavor
  • Mongo guisado — stewed mung beans with pork and malunggay. Simple, filling, very good
  • Tortang talong — grilled eggplant omelette served with rice

Cost at a carenderia: ₱60–120 for a full meal with rice and one or two viands.

Breakfast on Bantayan Island

Filipino breakfast revolves around silog combinations — garlic fried rice (sinangag) and a fried egg with a protein. On Bantayan, that protein is almost always danggit.

  • Danggit breakfast: Crispy fried dried rabbitfish, garlic rice, fried egg, and sliced tomatoes with salted egg. The definitive Bantayan Island breakfast — ₱80–120 at any local morning eatery
  • Bangsilog / tapsilog / longsilog: Bangus (milkfish), tapa (cured beef), or longganisa (local sausage) with sinangag and itlog — ₱80–150
  • Buko juice: Fresh young coconut juice served cold from the coconut. ₱30–50. Essential on a hot day

Street Food and Snacks

  • Fishball and squid ball — skewered, deep fried, with sweet or spicy sauce. ₱1–3 per piece
  • Barbecue — pork skewers in sweet soy glaze grilled over charcoal. Evening vendors typically set up from 5:00 PM. ₱15–25 per stick
  • Puso — hanging rice cooked in woven palm leaves, diamond-shaped. ₱5–10 each. Uniquely Visayan
  • Halo-halo — shaved ice with sweetened beans, jellies, leche flan, and ube ice cream. ₱50–100
  • Banana cue — sugar bananas fried in brown sugar on a skewer. ₱10–15

Buying Danggit to Take Home

Bantayan's dried danggit is sold throughout Santa Fe — you'll smell the drying racks before you see them. Prices typically run ₱200–400 per pack depending on size and quality. Buying directly from households that dry their own fish gives you the freshest product at the best price.

The Bantayan public market in Bantayan town (about 20 minutes by scooter from Santa Fe) has the widest selection of local products including fresh fish, native vinegar, dried goods, and produce. The morning market starts at 5:00 AM.

Daily Food Budget on Bantayan Island

  • Ultra-budget (carenderia every meal): ₱150–250/day
  • Mid-range (mix of carenderias and beachfront restaurants): ₱400–700/day
  • Comfortable (beachfront dining, fresh seafood, desserts): ₱800–1,500/day

Practical Food Tips

  • Cash only — most restaurants and all street food stalls on Bantayan do not accept cards
  • Opening hours — carenderias: 10:30 AM–2:00 PM. Beachfront restaurants: 7:00 AM–9:00 PM (varies)
  • Drinking water — do not drink tap water. Bottled water ₱15–25 per 500ml. Refilling stations charge ₱1–5 per liter
  • Best value — a grilled fresh fish at a market eatery or carenderia will almost always beat a tourist-facing restaurant for price, freshness, and authenticity
